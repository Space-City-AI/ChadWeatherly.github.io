01 / Research

Continual Anomaly Detection Under Industrial Drift

Anomaly detectors are usually evaluated on static test sets. Production lines are not static: tools wear, materials change, setups vary. My ECCV 2026 paper benchmarks continual anomaly detection under realistic industrial drift and shows how far standard methods fall under those conditions, several to near chance. The work that holds up combines foundation-model features with memory banks, evaluated with drift as the default rather than the exception.

Self-Supervised Anomaly Detection

Current work. Inspection data is mostly unlabeled and defects are rare by definition, so I pretrain representations on normal production images and detect deviations from them. A particular interest is the split between structural defects (scratches, dents, contamination) and logical errors: missing, misplaced, or wrong parts. Most detectors catch the first kind and miss the second. Evaluated on MVTec-AD, MVTec-LOCO, and VisA.

Anomaly Detection on Edge Hardware

Inspection has to run where the parts are. I design for small models (under 20 million parameters) running on NVIDIA Jetson and Raspberry Pi-class hardware at line speed, with no network connection to fall back on.

02 / Industry Work

I take on industry work through OffNominal Vision, my consulting practice. The common thread is software that has to run in production, on site, not as a demo.

X9 Inc. & MachNine: Manufacturing Software

2025 – present

Contract software engineer for a Houston-area manufacturing group. I build and maintain the systems their shops run on:

  • ShopFloorScheduler: production scheduling software, in active development toward a full ERP covering quoting, orders, inventory, and shipping.
  • RFID operator stations: Rust applications on Raspberry Pi kiosks that track parts and jobs at each work cell.
  • X-ray inspection stations: image acquisition and inspection software for automated quality checks.
  • Floor systems: the server, shop-floor displays, and WhatsApp-based reporting that tie the above together.

Wellmatics Engineering: X-Ray Inspection

2023 – 2025

Built a deep-learning pipeline for automated X-ray inspection of manufactured products across multiple product lines. The system reduced product failures by roughly 95% and cut manufacturing time by about 10%.
